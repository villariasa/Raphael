Raphael · Voice Input · Latency Design

End of speech to action in ~0.6 seconds — on a warm model.

Speaking is only faster than typing if the pipeline is. The architecture spends its latency budget deliberately: a persistent SenseVoice sidecar keeps the model warm, tight VAD endpointing decides when you've stopped talking, and the common "open X" commands skip the LLM entirely. Everything downstream is the pipeline a typed command already uses.

Why the sidecar stays warm

✗ Cold — reload model per utterance

+3–10 s

A one-shot Python call reloads SenseVoiceSmall from disk every single command. The transcription itself is fast; the load is what kills it. This is the trap to avoid.

✓ Warm — persistent sidecar

~250 ms

Main spawns the sidecar once, the model loads once and stays resident. Every utterance after that is just inference. This single decision is the whole fast-response story.

The levers, by impact

1

Warm model, persistent sidecar

Load once, keep resident. Removes a 3–10 s reload from every command.

Impact · decisive
2

Tight VAD endpointing

Detect end-of-speech in ~300 ms (Silero) instead of a long fixed silence timeout. Biggest perceived lag.

Impact · high
3

Regex fast-path skips the LLM

"open chrome", "search google for…" plan in ~1 ms. Only open-ended conversation hits Ollama.

Impact · high on common cmds
4

SenseVoiceSmall + 16 kHz mono

Feed the model its native fast path. ~5× faster than Whisper-Small at the same job.

Impact · medium
5

GPU when present, CPU fallback

Auto-detect CUDA; degrade to CPU without failing. Cuts STT further on capable machines.

Impact · hardware-dependent
6

Interim transcript (later)

SenseVoice isn't streaming, but a short rolling window could show "heard so far…" text. Deferred.

Impact · perceived only
